# Microeconomics: Notes on Cost

## 1. Meaning of Cost

* **General Meaning:** Cost is the money expenditure incurred by a firm in the production of a commodity.
* **Economic Meaning:** In economics, the total cost of production is the sum of explicit costs and implicit costs.

## 2. Types of Costs

### Explicit Costs
* **Definition:** These are the cash payments that firms make to outsiders for their services or goods.
* **Examples:**
    * Rent
    * Wages paid to workers
    * Interest Payment

### Implicit Costs
* **Definition 1:** Implicit Costs are the costs of self-owned or self-employed resources.
* **Definition 2:** These are the estimated values of inputs supplied by the owner of the firm himself.
* **Examples:**
    * Owner's own building provided for production
    * Owner's own capital
    * The owner may act as a manager of his firm himself.
* For these productive services, the owner does not pay rent, interest, or salary to himself.

### Private Cost
* **Definition:** This refers to the cost incurred by an individual firm in producing a commodity.

---

## 3. Short-Run Costs: Fixed and Variable

### Total Fixed Cost (TFC)
* **Definition:** Fixed costs are the costs that do not change with a change in the level of output.
* These are incurred on fixed factors like machines and buildings.
* **Examples:** Rent of building, interest on past borrowings, municipal taxes, insurance premium.
* **Other Names:** These costs are also called supplementary costs or overhead cost.
* **TFC Curve:** The TFC curve is a straight line parallel (11) to the x-axis, as TFC remains constant at all levels of output.

### Total Variable Cost (TVC)
* **Definition:** These are the costs which vary directly with the change in the level of output.
* These costs are incurred on variable inputs.
* **Examples:** Cost incurred on raw material, power, fuel, wages of temporary labour, and wear & tear of machines.
* **Other Names:** These costs are also known as prime cost or direct cost.
* **TVC Curve:** The TVC curve is upward sloping, which indicates that TVC increases with increasing output.

> **Key Note:** Fixed costs are available only in the short run. In the long run, all costs are variable.

### Comparison: Fixed vs. Variable Cost

| BASIS | FIXED COST | VARIABLE COST |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Changes Acc. to Prod[uction]** | Fixed cost does not increase or decrease with an increase or decrease in the level of prod[uction]. | It inc[reases] or decreases with an inc[rease] or decrease in the level of prod[uction]. |
| **Changes Acc. to Period** | It can't change during a short period. | It can be changed during the short period. |
| **Curve** | The Fixed Cost Curve is 11 [parallel] to X-axis. | Variable Cost Curve is upward sloping. |
| **Zero at Zero Level [of Output]**| Fixed Cost can't be zero, even when prod[uction] is stopped. | Variable cost is zero when prod[uction] is stopped. |
| **Stopped or Continue [Prod.]** | Prod[uction] may continue even at the loss of fixed cost during the short period. | Prod[uction] will be stopped when [variable cost] is not met during the short period. |
| **Examples** | Rent of factory, Salary of permanent employee, Insurance Premium. | Expenditure on purchase of raw material, power, fuel, wages of temporary labour. |

## 4. Total Cost (TC)

* **Definition:** Total cost is the cost of production of all the units of a commodity produced by a firm.
* **Formula:** Total Cost is the sum of total fixed cost and total variable cost.
    > TC = TFC + TVC

### Relationship between TFC, TVC, and TC
* The TFC curve is horizontal and parallel to the X-axis.
* TVC and TC curves are upward sloping because they increase with an increase in output.
* TC is the vertical summation of TFC and TVC curves.
* At 0 level of output, TC = TFC because there is no variable cost.
* After that, any change in TC is entirely due to the change in TVC.
* The TC curve and TVC curve have a similar shape.
* The TVC curve starts from the 0 level (origin), whereas the TC curve starts on the Y-axis from a point equal to the fixed cost.
* The vertical distance between the TC curve and TVC curve remains the same (equal to TFC).

---

## 5. Per-Unit Costs (Averages)

### Average Fixed Cost (AFC)
* **Definition:** It is the per-unit fixed cost of producing a commodity.
* **Formula:** It is calculated by dividing the TFC by the number of units of commodity produced.
    > AFC = Total Fixed Cost / No. of units produced

### Average Variable Cost (AVC)
* **Definition:** It is the per-unit variable cost of producing a commodity.
* **Formula:** It is calculated by dividing the total variable cost by the number of units produced.
    > Average Variable Cost = TVC / No. of units produced

### Average Total Cost (ATC)
* **Definition:** It is the per-unit cost of production of a commodity.
* **Formula:** It is calculated by dividing the total cost by the number of units produced.
    > ATC = TC / No. of units produced

---

## 6. Marginal Cost (MC)

* **Definition:** It is the addition to the Total Cost (TC) when an additional or extra unit of a commodity is produced.

---

## 7. Relationships Between Cost Curves

### MC and AVC Relationship
* When AVC is falling, MC < AVC.
* When AVC is rising, MC > AVC.
* *(Note: The document also states "At initial stage AVC = MC")*

### MC and ATC Relationship
* When AC (ATC) falls, MC < AC. The MC curve lies below the ATC curve.
* When AC (ATC) is constant, MC = AC. (This occurs at the minimum point of ATC).
* When AC (ATC) rises, MC > AC. The MC curve lies above the ATC curve.
* While rising, the MC curve cuts the ATC curve at its minimum point.
* When the MC curve is falling, it falls more rapidly than the ATC curve.

### Diagrammatic Relationships (ATC, AVC, MC)
* MC, AVC, and ATC curves are U-shaped because they fall up to a point and then start rising, which is related to the Law of Diminishing Returns.
* The MC curve intersects the AVC curve and the ATC curve at their minimum points.
* The vertical distance between the AVC and ATC curves is declining (as this distance represents AFC, which always falls).
* The AVC and ATC curves do not intersect each other because the difference between them is AFC, which is always positive.
