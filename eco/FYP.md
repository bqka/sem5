# 📘 **Five Year Plan – Notes**

## **1. Meaning**

A **Five Year Plan** is a government-designed, long-term economic development programme covering a period of **five years**.  
It sets **goals**, **policies**, and **resource allocation** to promote growth and improve living standards.

---

## **2. Objectives of Five Year Plans**

Common aims include:

- **Economic growth** (increase national income, GDP)
- **Reduction of poverty and unemployment**
- **Balanced regional development**
- **Development of agriculture & industry**
- **Expansion of infrastructure**
- **Social progress** (health, education, housing)
- **Reduce income inequalities**
- **Achieving self-reliance**

---

## **3. Features of Five Year Plans**

- Each plan covers **five years**.
- Based on **national priorities** of the time.
- Government allocates funds to different sectors.
- Targets are **quantitative** (numeric goals) and **qualitative** (policy improvements).
- Progress is reviewed periodically.
- Plans can be revised due to wars, droughts, economic crises, etc.

---

## **4. Five Year Planning in India (Overview)**

India launched its first plan in **1951**.  
The planning body was the **Planning Commission** (1950–2014).  
In **2015**, it was replaced by **NITI Aayog**, ending traditional Five Year Plans after the **12th Plan (2012–2017)**.

### **Major Plans (Brief):**

- **1st FYP (1951–56):** Agriculture, irrigation, food security
- **2nd FYP (1956–61):** Industrialisation, heavy industries
- **3rd FYP (1961–66):** Self-reliance, failed due to wars & drought
- **Plan Holiday (1966–69)**
- **4th FYP (1969–74):** Stability & growth
- **5th FYP (1974–79):** Poverty removal (Garibi Hatao)
- **6th FYP (1980–85):** Modernisation
- **7th FYP (1985–90):** Employment creation
- **8th FYP (1992–97):** Liberalisation, globalisation
- **9th FYP (1997–2002):** Growth with justice
- **10th FYP (2002–07):** Faster, broad-based growth
- **11th FYP (2007–12):** Inclusive growth
- **12th FYP (2012–17):** Faster, sustainable, more inclusive growth

After 2017, India switched to **Three-Year Action Agenda**, **7-Year Strategy**, and **15-Year Vision Document**.

---

## **5. Importance of Five Year Plans**

- Ensures **systematic and balanced development**
- Reduces wastage of resources
- Improves coordination across sectors
- Helps government plan budgets efficiently
- Encourages long-term national goals
- Leads to economic stability and progress

---

## **6. Limitations**

- Targets sometimes unrealistic
- Implementation delays
- Dependent on political stability
- Natural disasters or wars affect outcomes
