1. **Low per Capita Income and General Poverty:**
It is the main Indicator of the undeveloped economy. An UDC (Under Developed Country) is characterized by low per Capita Income, low standard of living, and poverty among the people. People live in misery, hunger, and diseases. India is an UDC and the per Capita Income was $62$ in some government record, but now according to 2009-10, the per Capita Income of India is $1045\$$ or about Rs. $55,000$ per annum, which is many times less than that in developed countries like U.S.A., Canada, Britain, etc. ($\$45000$). In India people take extremely poor food, and most of the people live below the poverty line.

2. **Unutilisation and Underutilisation of Natural Resources:** Another characteristic of UDC is that the natural resources in these countries are either unutilized or underutilized. In fact, these countries are not deficient in natural resources like land, water, mineral, forest, etc. The real problem with them is that these resources are not fully exploited or utilized due to many obstacles as lack of technical knowledge and primitive method of production. For example, there is about 90 million acres of wasteland. Again, the total hydropower potential is estimated at 4 million Kilowatt, but India hardly utilizes $21 \text{ Kilowatts}$. All this accounts for Indian poverty.

3. **Agriculture is the main Occupation.** In an UDC, a major percentage of the working population is engaged in agriculture. The greater dependence on Agriculture is due to the fact that the manufacturing sector and the service sector remain underdeveloped. According to the 1991 Census report, $66.8\%$ of the labour force in India was engaged in Agriculture. According to the 2001 Census, near about $55.7\%$ working force engaged in agriculture. According to Economic Survey 2012-13, near about $58.2\%$ working force engaged in Agriculture.

4. **Backward Agriculture:** In India, Agriculture is most backward. The average size of holding in India is very small. It is $2.6 \text{ hectare}$. These small holdings do not permit the use of mechanized method of production. So agriculture remains most backward and unproductive. It is carried out in old fashion and primitive and outdated methods are used, and as a result of it, there is low productivity in the Agriculture Sector.

5. **Population Pressure:** A high net rate of population increase is generally considered to be a characteristic of UDC. India is passing through this stage of population growth. In India, the main problem is that there is a high level of birth rate but a low level of death rate. This death rate in India has fallen because of the spread of medical facilities and use of preventive and vaccinative medicines. Death rate which was about $27.4$ per thousand during 1941-51 came down to $7.3$ per thousand in 2009. As compared to this, the birthrate did not come down at all. In 1941-51, the birth rate was $39.9$ per thousand, and in 2009, it was $22.65$ per thousand. This had led to a situation in which the rate of growth of population rises.

6. **Unemployment and underemployment:** Presence of unemployment and underemployment is another notable characteristic of underdeveloped countries. The unemployment is not voluntary but involuntary. Some people are ready to work, but work is not available for them. In India, both unemployment and disguised unemployment are on the increase. Near about $39.3$ million people are unemployed or underemployed.

7. **Economic Burden of Dependants:** Rising population increases the number of children and hence increases the burden of dependants in the country. A higher percentage of children demands higher expenditure on nutrition, medical care, and education. According to 1991 census, the ratio of dependants to the worker (productive worker) was $62:38$ as against $57:43$ according to 1961 Census. This shows that the burden of dependants is increasing in India.

8. **Low level of Technology:** Like another UDC, industry suffers from a low level of technology. Due to lack of Technology Know-how, much of the capital is wasted in the process of production, which results in a higher Capital Output Ratio. There is also scarcity of economic overheads on account of the absence of development. India has continued to use old and primitive methods in agriculture, industries, mining, etc.. Thus, these are the main features of underdeveloped economy OR characteristics of the Indian economy.

**What is Economic Growth and Economic Development. How can you Differentiate between them?**

**Answer:** Economic Growth: It has been defined as an increase in real terms of the output of goods and services that is sustained over a long period of time, measured in terms of Value Added. Economic growth is a process whereby an economy's real national income increases over a long period of time.

### **Economic Growth**

Economic growth refers to a sustained increase in the **real output of goods and services** in an economy over a period of time. It is usually measured in terms of **real national income**, **real GDP**, or **value added**. Growth focuses on **quantitative changes**—how much the economy produces.

**Key points:**

- Increase in real national income or output.
- Quantitative in nature.
- Can occur even if benefits do not improve people’s lives.
- Measured by GDP, GNP, or national income.
- Does not necessarily ensure improved living conditions.

---

### **Economic Development**

Economic Development refers to a **continuous improvement in the economic, social, and political conditions** of a country. It includes not only increases in income and output, but also improvements in **living standards**, **education**, **health**, **employment opportunities**, and **reduction of poverty**.  
Economic development is therefore **qualitative as well as quantitative**. It focuses on improving the **quality of life** of people.

**Key points:**

- Improvement in living standards (health, education, income).
- Qualitative and quantitative improvements.
- Includes reduction in poverty, inequality, and unemployment.
- Measured by indicators like HDI, literacy rate, life expectancy, per capita income.
- Focuses on both economic and social progress.
