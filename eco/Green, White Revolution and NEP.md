## 🚜 Green Revolution (New Agricultural Strategy)

The Green Revolution, or the new strategy for agricultural development, was implemented for the first time in India in **1966-67**. It aimed at securing a rapid increase in **food production** in the shortest possible time.

### **Key Components**

The strategy was based on the use of:
* **High Yielding Varieties (HYV) of Seeds:** Use of high yielding exotic and hybrid varieties of seeds.
* **Chemical Fertilizers:** Use of chemical fertilizers.

### **Effects of the Green Revolution**

The effects are categorized into Economic Effects and Sociological Impact.

#### **1. Economic Effects**

The two important economic effects were a sharp rise in agricultural production and an increase in productivity.

* **Increase in Agricultural Production:** There was a sharp rise in agricultural production immediately after implementation.
    * **Concentration on Wheat:** In the initial stages, the gains were largely cornered by the **wheat crop**, and only to a very little extent by the rice crop.
    * **Second Phase:** In the second phase, the revolution spread to other crops like rice, cotton, jute, and sugarcane, which registered a significant improvement, while wheat maintained an upward trend.
* **Increase in Productivity:** The increase in productivity resulted from the adoption of intensive agricultural practices. Agricultural productivity, measured as yield per hectare, increased by about **90%** between 1967-68 and 2001-02.

#### **2. Sociological Impact**

The Green Revolution pushed up the level of income in the rural sector due to increased production and price support measures. However, it had two discomforting features:

* **Personal Inequalities:** It promoted personal inequalities and widened the already existing gulf between the rich and the poor in the rural sector.
    * The modern technology was expensive, requiring a **"package approach"** with a number of inputs. Small farmers were often ignored by extension personnel and were denied the advantage of the new technology.
* **Regional Disparities:** It was responsible for widening regional disparities, a consequence of shifting the strategy from "something everywhere" to "everything somewhere".
    * Reasons for differential growth rates included differences in availability of water/irrigation, levels of fertilizer use, flow of information about HYV seeds, and availability of complementary inputs/pesticides.

### **Criticism of Green Revolution**

* It was initially limited to wheat, maize, and bajra. Progress in major commercial crops like oilseeds, cotton, and jute was very slow.
* More than 75% of the population still depends on land.
* The average holding is small (hardly two to three hectares).
* The yield per acre is much lower than in advanced countries.

## 🥛 **White Revolution (New Dairy Development Strategy)**

The **White Revolution**, also known as the new strategy for dairy development, began in India with the launch of **Operation Flood in 1970**.  
Its main objective was to achieve a rapid increase in **milk production** and to create a nationwide system for milk procurement, processing, and distribution.

---

# **Key Components**
 The strategy was based on the use of:
- **Dairy Cooperatives:** Formation of village-level milk producers’ cooperatives to collect and market milk in an organized manner.
- **Modern Dairy Technology:** Use of improved cattle breeds, artificial insemination, veterinary services, cattle feed, and modern processing and pasteurization facilities.

---

# **Effects of the White Revolution**

The effects can be grouped into **Economic Effects** and **Sociological Impact**.

## **1. Economic Effects**

The two most important economic effects were a sharp rise in milk production and an expansion of the dairy industry.

- **Increase in Milk Production:**  
    There was a significant rise in India’s milk production after Operation Flood.  
    _India became the largest milk producer in the world._
    - **Initial Focus:** In the early stages, the main gains were concentrated in states like **Gujarat, Punjab, and Haryana** where cooperatives were strong.
    - **Second Phase:** In the later phases, the revolution spread to other states, improving production of milk and milk products while maintaining the growth already achieved in leading states.
- **Expansion of Dairy Infrastructure:**  
    The rapid increase in production was supported by modern dairy plants, chilling centers, cold-storage chains, and veterinary health services.  
    The organized dairy sector, including **AMUL**, expanded rapidly.

---

## **2. Sociological Impact**

The White Revolution raised the level of income in the rural sector by providing farmers with daily cash income from milk sales. However, it also had two notable features:

- **Reduction of Rural Poverty but Unequal Gains:**  
    Although rural incomes increased, the benefits were not evenly distributed.
    - Farmers with better cattle breeds and more resources benefitted more.
    - Small and marginal farmers sometimes lacked access to quality inputs.
- **Regional Disparities:**  
    The development was uneven across regions—a result of shifting from a policy of “some support everywhere” to “intensive support in selected areas.”  
    Differences in cooperative strength, veterinary services, fodder availability, and access to technology led to varying rates of growth among states.

---

# **Criticism of the White Revolution**

- In its initial stages, it benefited mainly cooperative-dominant regions; progress in backward and tribal areas was slow.
- A large percentage of farmers still lack access to quality fodder, veterinary services, and improved cattle breeds.
- Average milk yield per animal remains lower than in many advanced dairy-producing countries.
- Issues such as inadequate cold-chain facilities and fluctuating market prices continue to affect farmers.

## 🏛️ New Industrial Policy (NEP) 1991

The government announced a New Industrial Policy on **July 24, 1991**, marking a sharp departure from the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956. Its basic philosophy was summed up as **"Continuity with change"**.

### **Objectives**

The objectives of the New Industrial Policy included:
1.  To consolidate the strengths built up over the last four decades of planning and build on the gains already made.
2.  To correct the weaknesses that may have crept into the industrial structures.
3.  To maintain a sustained growth in productivity and gainful employment.
4.  To attain international competitiveness.

### **Key Highlights**

#### **A. Industrial Licensing Policy**
* Industrial licensing was **abolished** for all projects except for a short list of industries related to security, strategy, and social concerns.
* Industries still subject to compulsory licensing included distillation of alcoholic drinks, tobacco products, electronic aerospace/defence equipment, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals, and drugs/pharmaceuticals.
* **Public Sector Exclusivity:** Only three industrial groups where security and strategy concerns predominated were reserved exclusively for the public sector: Atomic energy and Railway transport (though private capital was allowed limited entry in railways).

#### **B. Foreign Investment**
* **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI):** Automatic approval is available for FDI in almost all sectors, with automatic approval available for 50%, 51%, 74%, and over 100% in specified industry groups.
* **Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB):** Constituted to negotiate with large international firms and approve direct foreign investment in select areas.

#### **C. Foreign Technology Agreement**
* Automatic permission is given for foreign technology agreements in high productivity industries up to a lumpsum payment of **\$2 million**.
* Royalty payments were limited to **5%** for domestic sales and **8%** for exports, subject to total payments not exceeding 8% of sales over a 10-year period.

#### **D. Public Sector**
* The Public Sector's role was to be reviewed with a view to focusing it on Strategic, Technical, and essential Infrastructure.
* Chronically sick Public Enterprises unlikely to be turned around would be referred to the Board of Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR) for the formulation of revival schemes.
* **Disinvestment:** A part of the government's shareholding in the public sector would be offered to mutual funds, financial institutions, the general public, and workers to raise resources and encourage public participation.

#### **E. MRTP (Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practice) Act, 1969**
* The MRTP Act was amended to remove the limits of assets in respect of MRTP companies and dominant undertakings.
* Provisions related to concentration of economic power and pre-entry restrictions for expansion or amalgamation were removed.
* Emphasis was placed on controlling and regulating monopolistic, restrictive, and false trade practices.
