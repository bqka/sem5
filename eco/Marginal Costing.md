### **1. Core Cost & Revenue Relationships**

These formulas define the building blocks of marginal costing.

| Formula                                                            | Description                                                                                                                       |
| :----------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Total Cost** = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost                        | The total expenditure incurred in producing goods or services.                                                                    |
| **Contribution** = Sales - Variable cost                           | The revenue remaining after deducting variable costs; this amount contributes towards covering fixed costs and generating profit. |
| **Contribution** = Fixed cost + Profit                             | An alternative way to view contribution: the sum required to cover fixed costs and achieve the target profit.                     |
| **Marginal Cost** = Variable Cost                                  | By definition, the marginal cost is equal to the variable cost.                                                                   |
| **Fixed Cost** = Total Cost - Variable Cost                        | Calculates costs that remain constant regardless of production volume.                                                            |
| **Contribution per Unit** = Selling Price - Variable Cost per Unit | The contribution generated by selling a single unit.                                                                              |

---

### **2. Profit/Volume Ratio (P/V Ratio)**

The P/V ratio measures the rate at which contribution is earned on sales. A higher ratio indicates higher profitability.

$$
\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\text{Contribution}}{\text{Sales}} \times 100 \text{}
$$

$$
\text{P/V Ratio} = \frac{\text{change in Profit}}{\text{change in Sales}} \text{}
$$

---

### **3. Break-Even Point (BEP)**

The break-even point is the level of sales (units or value) where **Total Revenue = Total Cost**, meaning profit is zero.

#### **A. BEP in Units (Output)**

$$
\text{BEP (Units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}} \text{}
$$

#### **B. BEP in Sales Value (Amount)**

This can be calculated using either Contribution (C) or P/V Ratio.

$$
\text{BEP (Sales)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} \times 100 \text{}
$$

*Alternatively, using Unit Price:*
$$
\text{BEP (Sales)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}} \times \text{Selling Price per Unit} \text{}
$$

---

### **4. Sales for Desired Profit**

This determines the revenue (or units) required to cover fixed costs and achieve a specific profit target.

#### **A. Sales in Units**

$$
\text{Units for Desired Profit} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Desired Profit}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}} \text{}
$$

#### **B. Sales in Value (Amount)**

$$
\text{Sales for Desired Profit} = \frac{\text{Fixed Cost} + \text{Desired Profit}}{\text{Profit Volume Ratio}} \times 100 \text{}
$$

---

### **5. Margin of Safety (MoS)**

The Margin of Safety is the excess of actual or desired sales over the break-even sales. It represents the cushion against losses.

$$
\text{Margin of Safety} = \text{Sales at desired profit} - \text{Sales at break even point} \text{}
$$

$$
\text{Margin of Safety} = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{P/V Ratio}} \times 100 \text{}
$$
