Sustainable development is a modern variant of the concept of development. It refers to that process of economic development which aims at maintaining the quality of life of both present and future generations. It emphasises development without environmental degradation. Environmental degradation includes: (i) environmental pollution and (ii) exploitation of natural resources. The concept of sustainable development stresses that the process of development should be such that it causes minimum possible environmental pollution as well as minimum possible exploitation of natural resources. Achieving higher and higher rates of production along with greater and greater environmental pollution simply implies a situation of growth or development with higher and higher social cost. This makes no sense. Likewise, achieving higher and higher rates of production through excessive exploitation of natural resources implies a situation when present development is achieved by lowering the production capacity of the future generation. It is a situation of present development at the cost of future development which is ridiculous.

**Environment**
Environment means all those situations and surroundings which influence the life and development of the human beings. Environment includes non-living things like soil and the climate, and living things like animals, creatures, plants and trees, etc. Smoke and other injurious emissions from factories and transport vehicles often lead to pollution of environment.

**Sustainable Development** is a process that maximises for present and future generations long-term net gains resulting from economic development. It is that process of development which meets the needs of the present generation without reducing the ability of the future generation to meet their own needs.

In the words of Robbert Repetto, "Sustainable Development is a development strategy that manages all natural resources and human resources as well as financial and physical assets for increasing long term wealth and well-being."

### Global Warning and the Process of Development

'Global warming' now-a-days is a buzz-word. Everybody is talking about it. It relates to the rising global temperature due to environmental pollution resulting from rapid industrialisation and deforestation. Owing to emission of green house gases (carbon dioxide in particular), the earth's surface is becoming increasingly warmer, showing a rise $0.6^{\circ} \text{C}$ during the past century. Warming temperature escalates the melting process of polar ice, in turn, implying a rise in sea level. Consequently, ecological imbalance is impaired, inflicting greater incidence of natural calamities. Ultimately, it implies a threat to human life. The tall claim of growth and development of developed and developing countries at the cost of environmental degradation, ozone depletion (it refers to reduction in ozone, a protective layer in the atmosphere. This occurs owing to the excessive use of cooling substances in air conditioners and refrigerators) and depletion of natural resources is not sustainable. Development and growth of the present generation should not be at the cost of the generations to come. Realising the imminent danger, every country in the world is emphasising the need for sustainable development, the process of development that arrests the process of global warming.

### 2.1 Features of Sustainable Development

Main features of sustainable development are as under:
(1) **Sustained Rise in Real Per Capita Income and Economic Welfare:** There should be a sustained rise in real per capita income and economic welfare over time.

(2) **Rational Use of Natural Resources:** Sustainable development does not mean that natural resources should not be used at all. It simply means that natural resources should be rationally used in a manner such that they are not excessively exploited.

(3) **No Reduction in the Ability of Future Generations to Meet their Own Needs:** Sustainable development aims at making use of natural resources and environment for raising the existing standard of living in such a way as not to reduce ability of the future generations to meet their own needs. For example, if non-renewable sources of energy (like oil) are recklessly used to increase present production at the cost of our ability to produce in the future, it would not be in sync with the concept of sustainable development.

**A Necessary Clarification**
The concept of sustainable development does not suggest delimiting the process of growth and development. In other words, it should not be interpreted to mean that limited growth and development would spare more resources for the future generations. It only suggests rational utilisation of the use of natural resources in a manner such that what we achieve today should not be at the cost of what ought to be achieved tomorrow.

(4) **No Increase in Pollution:** Sustainable development discards (or does not approve of) those activities which, in order to maintain existing high standard of living, prove detrimental to natural resources and environment. According to this concept, one should desist from undertaking such activities that may increase pollution and adversely impact the quality of life of future generations.

### 2.2 Strategies for Sustainable Development

The concept of sustainable development does not suggest delimiting the process of growth and development. Sustainable development should not be interpreted to mean that a check on the pace of growth would imply a check on the exploitation of resources and also a check on environmental pollution. Sustainable development only suggests a judicious or optimum utilisation of resources and in a manner such that the pace of economic growth is sustained with inter-generational equity.

How can it be achieved? What are the possible strategies for achieving sustainable development? Following observations may be noted in this context:

(1) **Input Efficient Technology:** We are to devise such production technologies which are input efficient. It means more is produced per unit of input. This will reduce the exploitation of resources to meet the emerging needs.

(2) **Use of Environment-friendly Sources of Energy:** LPG and CNG are cleaner fuels and environment-friendly. The use of these fuels must be encouraged in place of petrol and diesel which emit huge amount of carbon-dioxide adding to the incidence of green house gases.

People in rural areas should be discouraged from using wood as a fuel. It causes deforestation and therefore, unwarranted depletion of resources. Instead, gobar gas plants may be developed as a source of domestic fuel.

(3) **Integrated Rural Development:** Integrated rural development must be the main concern of state planning. This will generate employment opportunities in rural areas, restricting migration to urban areas. If rural-urban migration is checked, transportation needs will automatically be reduced. Accordingly, one can expect a check on environmental pollution, besides a saving on fuels.

(4) **Convert Sunlight into Solar Energy and Solar Energy into Electricity:** Energy is the most critical input of production. While use of fossil fuels implies a direct depletion of non-renewable sources of energy, the use of coal is grossly unfriendly to environment as it emits lots of carbon-dioxide. In countries like India, sunlight is a rich natural gift and a rich source of energy which is both environment friendly as well as non-exhaustible. We have made only a modest beginning in harnessing sunlight as a source of solar energy. Also, conversion of solar energy into electricity is no longer a distant dream. But the technology is yet to become widely applicable.

Scientists need to focus more on it as it can offer a real breakthrough to countries like India where energy is a big bottleneck in the process of economic growth. Solar energy (and its conversion into electricity) is an effective answer not only to the problem of economic growth, but also to the problem of sustainable development.

(5) **Shift to Organic Farming:** Excessive use of chemical fertilizers, insecticides and pesticides have raised the crop yield. But at the cost of soil fertility, which means a loss of production capacity for the future generations. It is high time that we change over to organic farming which promotes soil-health rather than the plant-health.

(6) **Recycle the Wastes:** Rather than allowing the wastes (both industrial as well as household) to spread here and there and adding to environmental pollution, we must recycle them. Household waste can be recycled into compost and used as a manure for organic farming.

(7) **Stringent Laws on the Disposal of Chemical Effluents:** India is a land where laws are enacted, but seldom enforced. Chemical effluents from the industrial units have invariably been flowing into the streams and rivers, polluting water and taking a toll on aquatic life. Stringent laws and their strict enforcement is the need of the hour if we wish to save our environment.

(8) **Awareness to Conserve Natural Assets for Inter-generational Equity:** The Indians are highly conscious of inter-generational equity at the micro level. At the family level, we shall do our best to preserve our assets as a rich bequeath for the future generations. But this awareness is very bleak at the macro level, the national level. We are wary of conserving our natural wealth for future generations. Of course, there are examples like the 'Chipko' movement in the north and 'Appiko' movement in the south where people clung to the trees, forcing the woodcutters to retreat. But such examples are yet to culminate into a national awareness to save our environment and natural assets.

(9) **Public Means of Transport:** Public means of transport are to be rapid, comfortable and economical. Example: Metro in Delhi, though its services are yet to percolate to all parts of the city. Such means of transport must be extended to the sub-urban areas as well. This will drastically cut the private vehicular traffic in and around the metro cities, which is a major source of environmental pollution, air and noise pollution in particular.

Briefly, sustainable development is a process to be practised as a daily-life activity. It is not simply a specially designed programme or process of production. It is more about our awareness of the implications of what we are doing as producers and consumers. It is more about our concern for future generations and our social responsibilities. It involves **less of cost, and more of consciousness**. As producers, we are to be conscious of the fact that there could be environment-friendly techniques of production and environment-friendly techniques of disposal of the wastes. As consumers, we are to be conscious of the fact that there could be environment-friendly means of consumption and environment-friendly means of disposal of the garbage. Of course, the government should contribute its bit through enforcement of the laws relating to environment. Stressing our environment to serve us beyond its capacity would only mean reducing its capacity to serve us in the future. This is where the process of development fails to be sustainable.
